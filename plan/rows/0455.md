## Evidence

Follow-up of #434. Every save of a published definition adds a version of up to 1 MB and versions are never deleted, so the tables grow without a policy; the payload text is deliberately not in the audit trail, so the version table is the history and must not be pruned carelessly. Cache behaviour (hits, stamp reads) is invisible.

## Prompt

1) Owner decision on a retention policy (keep the published version and the last N retired versions or a minimum age; never prune a version that a draft was cloned from or that is the shipped version; legal hold for audited kinds). 2) A background job that prunes by the policy, with a dry-run report and an audit entry. 3) A size and growth report (rows and bytes per kind, largest definitions) on the definition manager (#449) and in the support bundle (#174). 4) Orphan cleanup of references of pruned versions. 5) Cache statistics (hits, misses, stamp reads) in the health dashboard. 6) Settings in env/*.env; tests.

## Requirement

Done when: with the policy set, a definition with 200 retired versions is pruned to the policy without touching the published, shipped or cloned-from versions; a dry run lists what would go; the report and cache statistics are visible; all covered by tests.
